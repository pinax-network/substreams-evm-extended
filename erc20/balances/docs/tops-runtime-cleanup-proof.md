# TOPS original-runtime cleanup proof

This host-only proof executes the complete captured TOPS runtime at `0xcdf52c0b13c24f32f1d8d4ec6356203a1ef0826a` through its ordinary `transfer` and `transferFrom` paths. The matrix supplies synthetic storage, origin, timestamp, gas words and two read-only external responses. It is not deployment, external dependency, package or holder qualification, and adds no ingestion candidate or production validation rule.

The original runtime and compiler artifacts retain the exact bindings in the [earlier TOPS source proof](tops-operation-proof.md). The old compiler report and inventory remain separately pinned historical records. Fresh preparation and execution must have the same complete current-source inventory, including compiled snapshots of the VM, optional context, source mapper, ABI/hash helpers, both drivers and tests. No new compiler run is claimed.

The optional context is available only through a new host entrypoint. Existing VM entrypoints, unsupported-opcode messages and execution JSON remain unchanged. It supplies an explicit address-sized ORIGIN, a full-width timestamp, exact ordered GAS reads, and at most two STATICCALL expectations. Each call matches its exact original-code PC, masked destination, gas operand, complete calldata and requested output width. Successful and reverted response buffers are separate typed values; a short copy preserves the remainder of output memory. Return-data copying checks the source range even at zero length. Scripts must be fully consumed before a return or revert can be accepted. Unexpected, missing or extra interactions are harness failures, including interactions inside a source try/catch. Every failure rolls back local committed storage and logs while retaining attempted effects.

The normal script is the captured pair `0x8ec426ffb466990ac36048ef5c90348783ea4272` at STATICCALL PC 7758 with `getReserves()`, followed by USDT `0x55d398326f99059ff775485246999027b3197955` at PC 8143 with `balanceOf(pair)`. Zero reserves take the source's early price-update return. Synthetic origin differs from the sender unless the source's explicit `inSwap` branch suppresses its independent price check. No pair or USDT code executes; gas words are not gas sufficiency, costs or fork qualification.

The independent expected-state model covers arrays of zero through six three-word LP records, expiration equality, the first unexpired cutoff even when later records are expired, overlapping forward compaction, tail clearing, repeated length stores and wrapped credit. An expired sum that wraps to zero suppresses cleanup; a nonzero wrapped sum performs it. Checked LP getter failures remain distinct. Ordinary transfers also cover zero amount, self-transfer, a zero recipient and wrapped recipient credit. Finite allowance changes occur after transfer effects and its log; a late underflow preserves the complete attempted prefix but commits nothing. MAX allowance omits that final write.

The saved block 123561227, transaction 63, call 14 control independently reconstructs all 23 captured stores: five three-word tail pops with five length updates, one credit update and two token balance writes. Its amount is 393140350383254688654 and its expired aggregate is 14594824580393536426. The LP owner is selected as synthetic origin, timestamp is chosen as the maximum captured expiry, and external responses/unrelated storage are synthetic. This is exact comparison of the saved local store sequence and calldata, not a full historical transaction replay.

Malformed return lengths and dirty ABI words, explicit external reverts, wrong/missing scripts, and the router try/catch boundary are refusal controls. Add/remove-liquidity branches retain their cleanup prefix before the next unsupported read and roll back. Their separate root 32 liquidity arithmetic is not qualified. The original constructor and other router, fee, reward and stateful-call paths remain outside the proof. The earlier extracted cleanup harness remains source-semantics-only; its ordering is not relabeled as deployed ordering.
